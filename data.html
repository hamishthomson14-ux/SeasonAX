Where the data comes from

  • Prices: daily closing prices from Yahoo Finance’s public chart data, fetched by TimingAX’s own servers. Where Yahoo provides dividend-adjusted closes, those are used; index levels such as the S&P 500 are price-only. Up to 20 years are fetched; seasonal statistics use the most recent 15.
  • Scheduled events: Federal Reserve rate decisions from the official FOMC calendar at federalreserve.gov; CPI and jobs-report dates from the release schedules at bls.gov.
  • Assets without enough real history are never shown with real-looking numbers: they’re labelled MODELLED (a sector pattern) or left out, depending on the page.

How the figures are calculated

  • Monthly average return: the change from one month’s last close to the next month’s last close, averaged for each calendar month over every year from January of the year 15 years ago to today — up to 16 observations per month, and the current month counts with its latest close so far. Assets with less history use the longest of a 10, 7, 5, 3 or 2-year window that gives every month enough observations. Monthly moves larger than 95% are excluded as data errors.
  • Win rate: the share of those years in which the month closed higher.
  • Tailwind / headwind: a month is a tailwind when its average is at least +0.8% and its win rate at least 60%, a headwind when its average is −0.5% or lower and its win rate 45% or lower; otherwise neutral.
  • Seasonal projection: the move over the same calendar window (30, 60 or 90 days from today) in each of the last 15 years, applied to the latest close; the chart shows the median path, the middle half of years and the full range. Only complete windows are used.
  • Leaderboard: this month’s average return for every asset with real data and at least 5 years of observations for the month.
