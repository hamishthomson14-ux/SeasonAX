    Is November Really the Best Month for Stocks? It Depends Where You Start Counting

    5 MIN READ · DATA TO NOVEMBER 2025 · PUBLISHED 28 OCTOBER 2026

    Every autumn the same headline comes round: November is the best month of the year for stocks. On the most recent 15 years of data, that’s true for the S&P 500. On 20 years it’s less clear-cut. And outside the US, the picture changes again.

    The 15-year view: November comes out on top

    From 2011 to 2025, the S&P 500 rose in 13 of 15 Novembers, with an average gain of +3.11%. That makes it the best of the 12 months in that window. The weakest month in the same window was September: an average of −1.29%, up in only 7 of 15 years.

    The Nasdaq Composite shows almost exactly the same thing: up in 87% of Novembers, averaging +3.22%, and again its best month.

    Add five years and the average drops by a third

    Stretch the window to 20 years (2006–2025) and the S&P 500’s November is up in 15 of 20 years, averaging +2.09%. Still a good month, but the average is about a third lower.

    The difference is mostly two years: November 2007 (−4.40%) and November 2008 (−7.48%), during the financial crisis. They sit outside the 15-year window and inside the 20-year one. A seasonal average depends on the period it covers, and one bad stretch can move it a long way. Any “best month” claim should tell you which years it’s based on.

    Even the good years vary a lot. The best November of the 20 was 2020 (+10.75%). Last year, 2025, the index rose just +0.13%.

    The FTSE 100 rose in fewer than half of the last 20 Novembers. One point of fairness: these are price returns, and the FTSE 100 is a high-dividend index, so its total return would look better.

    Crypto: big average, bigger range

    Bitcoin’s November average since 2014 is +7.51%, but that hides a huge spread: +58.21% in 2017 and −36.41% in 2018. It rose in 7 of 12 Novembers, and last November (2025) it fell 17.49%. With swings that size, the average says very little about any single year. Bitcoin in November, every year.

    Single stocks

    Nvidia rose in 15 of the last 20 Novembers, averaging +5.56%. But its worst was −22.41% (2018), and last November it fell 12.59%. Tesla has risen in 11 of 16 Novembers since 2010.

    What to take from this

    • November has been a strong month for US indices on both 15 and 20 years of data, but “the best month” holds on the 15-year window, not on every window.
    • The average hides the range. Look at the individual years, especially the bad ones.
    • Outside the US, the pattern is much weaker.
    • Seasonality is context, not a forecast. Our own out-of-sample track record shows how often seasonal signals matched what happened, and how often markets rose anyway.
